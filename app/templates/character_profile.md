# Character profile - how to fill this in

> This is a reference template, not a character. Copy it with `/doc new character <name>`, then replace the
> prompts in each section. Keep the two status rules below as you write.
>
> **Two rules that keep a profile trustworthy**
> 1. Mark anything you have not decided as **OPEN**. Do not invent fixed facts to fill a gap.
> 2. Label where a detail came from: **author-decided**, **proposed** (a draft idea), or **research** (real-world
>    reference, not story canon). A profile that hides its uncertainty will mislead you later.

A good profile answers twelve things. Short, concrete notes beat long prose. Delete the guidance lines as you
replace them.

## 1. Identity
- **Name:** who they are called, plus any titles, aliases, or names others use.
- **Age / stage:** at the point this profile describes (a character changes; pick a moment).
- **Role in the story:** protagonist, foil, antagonist, mentor, and so on.
- **In one line:** the essence of this person in a single sentence.

## 2. Physical presence
- **Appearance:** only what you have decided. If eye color, height, or build are undecided, write OPEN.
- **Distinguishing features:** a scar, a way of dressing, something a reader remembers.
- **Bearing:** how they move and occupy a room (wary, commanding, self-effacing).

## 3. Background and history
- **Origin:** where and what they come from.
- **Formative events:** the two or three things that made them who they are.
- **What they lost or left behind:** the past that still pulls at them.

## 4. Inner life and personality
- **Temperament:** the everyday texture of how they think and feel.
- **Values:** what they will not compromise.
- **Contradictions:** the trait that fights another trait. This is where character lives.

## 5. Want versus need
- **Want (conscious goal):** what they are chasing and would name out loud.
- **Need (the real lesson):** what they actually have to learn, often the opposite of the want.
- The gap between these two is the engine of the arc.

## 6. Wound, lie, and fear
- **The wound:** the past hurt that still shapes their choices.
- **The lie they believe:** the false conclusion they drew from the wound.
- **Deepest fear:** what they will go to great lengths to avoid.

## 7. Relationships
- **Allies and loved ones:** who they trust, and why.
- **Rivals and antagonists:** who opposes them, and what that opposition is really about.
- **Who they change, and who changes them.**

## 8. Voice and mannerisms
- **How they speak:** rhythm, formality, what they say when cornered.
- **Habits and tics:** the small physical or verbal signatures that identify them.

## 9. Capabilities and limits
- **Skills and strengths:** what they are good at.
- **Powers, if any, and their cost:** every ability should have a price and a boundary.
- **Hard limits:** what they cannot or will not do. State these plainly.

## 10. Arc and transformation
- **Starting state:** who they are at the beginning.
- **Turning points:** the moments that force change.
- **Ending state:** who they become, or fail to become.

## 11. Role in theme
- **The idea they embody or test:** what question about the story's theme this character puts pressure on.

## 12. Open questions and status
- **Decided:** the facts you are committing to.
- **Open:** everything still undecided. List it here so it is never quietly invented.
- **Proposed:** draft ideas awaiting your approval.
- **Conflicts:** places where two drafts disagree; keep both until you choose.
